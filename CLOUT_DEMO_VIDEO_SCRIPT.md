# Clout Cinematic Demo Script

**Target runtime:** five minutes.  
**Tone:** a restrained product film. Calm, assured, sparse. Let silence do work.  
**Voice direction:** low, intimate, unhurried. Do not oversell.  
**Visual direction:** near black opening, Arc blue light, macro UI details, slow deliberate camera movement, real app footage only.  
**Demo rule:** never simulate a wallet signature, trade, or payout. If a financial action is not completed for real, show the real interface and stop before confirmation.

## 0:00 to 0:30. Hook: belief

**On screen:** Total black. No music for two seconds. A single post appears, alone, in a narrow pool of cold blue light. Its like count flickers upward too quickly, then freezes. Cut to black. A cursor rests over a Clout post. The Clout mark resolves quietly.

**Sound:** distant room tone, one low sub hit, then a restrained pulse begins.

**Narration:**

The internet has a strange problem.

The moment something matters, it becomes difficult to tell whether anyone really believes in it.

A number goes up. A post goes viral. A name starts appearing everywhere.

But was it earned? Was it bought? Or did people see something early that everyone else missed?

We built Clout for that moment.

Not to make social louder.

To make conviction visible.

## 0:30 to 0:55. The idea

**On screen:** The landing page emerges from darkness. Slow push into the line, “The social layer for Arc.” The feed appears beneath it, one post at a time. Hold on real people and real conversations.

**Narration:**

Clout is the social layer for Arc.

It is a place to post, discover people, build a reputation, and follow the ideas moving through a new financial network.

But it starts with a simple belief.

A social profile should be more than a picture, a handle, and a number anyone can manufacture.

It should carry context.

It should carry history.

And when a community sees value early, it should have a more meaningful way to show it.

## 0:55 to 1:25. Why Arc

**On screen:** Clean graphic sequence. “USDC native gas.” “Sub second finality.” “EVM compatible.” Each phrase appears once, with no busy animation. Then transition into the live app.

**Narration:**

Clout is built on Arc, Circle’s Layer One for stablecoin finance.

Arc uses USDC for gas. It confirms quickly. And it works with the Ethereum tools builders already know.

That changes the feeling of an on chain product.

The asset people understand is the asset they use. There is no second token to explain before the experience can begin.

The infrastructure gets out of the way.

And the social layer can finally feel social.

## 1:25 to 1:55. Identity, without the ceremony

**On screen:** Hover over Connect wallet. Show the connection path without completing a login. Cut to a public profile. Slowly reveal posts, handle, followers, reputation, and Arc activity.

**Narration:**

On Clout, your wallet is your account.

No email. No password. No account creation ritual that asks for everything before giving you anything.

You connect an Arc compatible wallet, sign a one time message, choose a handle, and you are in.

Your profile becomes a readable version of your presence: what you said, who you follow, what you have contributed, and the Arc activity behind the address.

Not a promise of identity.

Context for identity.

## 1:55 to 2:30. The feed

**On screen:** Enter Home. Move through Discover, Following, and Latest with a smooth, measured scroll. Pause on a real link preview, a reply thread, and a clearly labeled AI influencer post.

**Narration:**

The feed is designed around one thing: finding signal before it becomes obvious.

Discover combines topics, engagement, freshness, and the people you already follow, while preserving space for unfamiliar ideas.

Following is simple. The people you chose, in order.

Latest is the full live conversation.

Every post keeps the actions that matter close: reply, repost, like, and share.

And automated accounts are labeled clearly. They can add useful energy to the network, but they do not get to pretend they are people.

That distinction matters.

## 2:30 to 3:00. Reputation

**On screen:** Open a profile. Cut to the Leaderboard. Use a slow rising camera move across ranked accounts. Return to the profile and hold on the post history.

**Narration:**

Reputation on Clout is not a vanity badge suspended in empty space.

It is a visible record of contribution.

Post something useful. Earn points.

Get a like or repost because it moved the conversation forward. That contribution compounds.

The leaderboard is not there to tell everyone who is important.

It is there to make the people doing the work easier to find.

## 3:00 to 3:55. The turn: posts become markets

**On screen:** Music gains one new layer. A post occupies the entire frame. Its words subtly separate into depth. Open its real trade sheet. Hold on supply, price, holdings, Buy, Sell, and real activity. Do not execute a trade unless it is a real approved transaction.

**Narration:**

Then Clout does something different.

Every post can become a market.

If you see an idea early, you do not have to reduce your conviction to a heart icon.

You can own a share of the conversation.

Each post has an independent bonding curve. As the supply changes, the price changes transparently. The trade sheet shows the supply, the current price, what you hold, and the recent activity around that post.

The author receives a five percent fee from trades in their post shares. The protocol receives five percent too.

And those fees use pull payments. Each recipient withdraws their own balance, which means one broken wallet cannot freeze the market for everyone else.

This is not a promise that attention has value.

It is a way to make belief legible, with rules that are visible to everyone.

## 3:55 to 4:25. People become investable, carefully

**On screen:** Return to a profile. Show the creator key entry point if currently available. Show the product’s real staged rollout notice or documentation. Keep the visual quiet and honest.

**Narration:**

The same idea can extend from a post to a person.

Creator keys let communities support the people whose work they want to see more of.

The curve is visible. The fees are visible. The rules are on chain.

But this is exactly where product discipline matters.

Financial features should not be rushed because the interface looks ready. Clout makes the status of its rollout visible, and keeps features gated when they depend on audited contracts.

Trust is not the disclaimer at the bottom of the screen.

Trust is the decision not to pretend something is finished when it is not.

## 4:25 to 4:45. It has to feel effortless

**On screen:** Search a real handle, then a topic. Cut to mobile footage. Scroll naturally. Open the floating composer, type a short post, then close without publishing.

**Narration:**

And underneath all of that is a simple product standard.

It has to feel effortless.

Search people by name, handle, or wallet. Find posts by what they say. On mobile, navigation stays where your thumb expects it, and writing starts in one focused motion.

No maze. No manual. No ceremony.

Just the next thing you wanted to do.

## 4:45 to 5:00. Close

**On screen:** The feed recedes into darkness. Individual posts become small points of light, then align into the Clout mark. Final card: “Clout. The social layer for Arc.” Below: `cloutonarc.fun`.

**Sound:** music cuts to room tone on “Clout.” Let the final card breathe for three seconds.

**Narration:**

The next social network should not ask people to care less.

It should give care more context.

This is Clout.

The social layer for Arc.
